01

Building the identity behind the name.

My work sits at the intersection of entity SEO, structured data and digital reputation. In practice, that means helping search engines understand what an entity actually is — and making sure the information a person, founder or company puts out there tells one clear, consistent story.

The starting point is usually a question: when someone searches for this person or this company, what does the engine actually find? Often the answer is fragments — a name on one site, a different spelling on another, a profile with no context, an outdated mention that outranks the official page.

The approach is entity-first: research, website optimization, profile development, information alignment, schema recommendations and authority building. The aim is clarity, consistency and long-term discoverability — the kind of foundation that keeps working after any single campaign ends.

04

How an entity becomes findable

Most people arrive at a name already scattered — a website, a few profiles, a mention or two. The work is not creating more noise. It is arranging what is real into a shape a search engine can actually read.

  1. 1Real entity
  2. 2Official website
  3. 3Official profiles
  4. 4Professional platforms
  5. 5Third‑party sources
  6. 6Structured information

Outcome Search engines can more clearly understand and associate information with the entity.

What the work controls

  • The website and its content
  • Information published on profiles
  • Structured data implementation
  • Search Console setup and monitoring
  • Research, audits and reporting

What Google controls

  • Whether an entity is recognized
  • Whether a Knowledge Panel appears
  • What information is displayed
  • When systems update

A Knowledge Panel is not something a specialist can create or guarantee. Google decides whether one appears and what it contains. What this work does is improve the legitimate information environment around an entity — which is the part that can actually be influenced.
